## Framework 97: Memetic‑Thermodynamic‑Gödelian Ontology

**Core Premise**  
Memes (units of cultural transmission) obey a thermodynamic second law: total meme entropy increases over time, but Gödelian undecidable memes act as Maxwell’s demons, locally reversing entropy by selecting which memes replicate. Cultural evolution is driven by paradox‑carrying memes.

**Mathematical Foundation**

- Second law: \( dS_{\text{memetic}}/dt \geq 0 \) for closed meme pool.  
- Gödelian meme: a meme that asserts its own unprovability within the meme’s host logic.  
- Maxwell demon operator: \( \hat{D}_{\text{Gödel}} \) that reduces local entropy by selectively amplifying undecidable memes.  
- Fluctuation theorem for memes: \( \frac{P(\Delta S = -A)}{P(\Delta S = +A)} = e^{-A/k_B T_{\text{culture}}} \) (rare local decreases allowed).

**Synthesis**  
Memes are the fundamental particles of culture. Their dynamics obey an entropic law—left alone, cultural diversity (meme entropy) increases. However, certain memes—those that are self‑referential and undecidable (e.g., “this statement is false”)—can act as Maxwell’s demons. They selectively allow some memes to replicate and others to die, locally decreasing entropy at the cost of increasing it globally. This explains why paradoxes (koans, ironic memes, recursive jokes) are powerful cultural engines: they pump entropy against the gradient.

**Implications**  
- Viral memes are low‑entropy, highly ordered; they decrease local meme entropy.  
- Gödelian memes (paradoxes) are cultural Maxwell demons.  
- The internet is an open meme‑thermodynamic system; its total entropy increases, but local pockets (subreddits, fandoms) can decrease.  
- Cancellation culture is a high‑entropy state; forgiveness is a Gödelian demon that locally reverses it.
